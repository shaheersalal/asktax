Section 01

Income Tax — Individuals & Salaried Persons

The income tax slabs for salaried individuals under the Finance Act 2024 are:

Annual Taxable Income (PKR)Tax Rate
Up to 600,0000%
600,001 – 1,200,0005%
1,200,001 – 2,200,000Rs 30,000 + 15% above Rs 1.2M
2,200,001 – 3,200,000Rs 180,000 + 25% above Rs 2.2M
3,200,001 – 4,100,000Rs 430,000 + 30% above Rs 3.2M
Above 4,100,000Rs 700,000 + 35% above Rs 4.1M

These rates apply to salaried income. Business/other income uses a different rate card. Rates are subject to change in each annual Finance Act.

Non-salaried individuals (business owners, freelancers, sole proprietors) are taxed under a separate slab structure. Income up to Rs 600,000 is exempt. Between Rs 600,001 and Rs 1,200,000 the rate is 15%. The rate escalates to 20%, 25%, 30%, and 35% at higher income bands. Non-salaried persons also pay advance tax in quarterly installments under Section 147.

Agricultural income is exempt from federal income tax under the Income Tax Ordinance 2001, as agriculture is a provincial subject under the Constitution. However, provinces levy their own Agricultural Income Tax (AIT). Punjab and Sindh both have AIT legislation. Additionally, agricultural income is considered when calculating the effective rate of federal tax on non-agricultural income (the rate-on-rate method).

Super tax was introduced under Section 4C of the Income Tax Ordinance. It applies to high-income individuals and companies. For tax year 2023 onward, individuals and AOPs with income exceeding Rs 150 million pay super tax at progressive rates ranging from 1% to 10% depending on the income tier. Banks pay super tax at 4%. The super tax is in addition to the regular income tax liability.

Rental income is taxed separately under a fixed rate scheme. Under Section 155, tax is withheld at source by the tenant if the annual rent exceeds Rs 1.2 million. The withholding rate is 15% for filers. Non-filers pay 20%. Gross rent minus allowable deductions (mortgage interest, ground rent, insurance) equals the net assessable amount. Losses from one property can be offset against income from other properties.

Yes, freelance income is taxable in Pakistan. Freelancers earning from foreign clients in foreign exchange may benefit from a reduced tax rate — FBR has provided incentives for IT and IT-enabled services exporters including a 0.25% fixed tax on export proceeds (Section 154A). Freelancers must register their NTN, file annual income tax returns, and declare all income. Payments received through platforms like Upwork, Fiverr, or Payoneer are all taxable income.

Section 02

Filing Returns & NTN Registration

NTN registration can be completed online via FBR's IRIS portal (iris.fbr.gov.pk). Steps: (1) Visit IRIS and select "Registration for Unregistered Person". (2) Enter your CNIC number. (3) Complete the registration form with your address, contact details, and income source. (4) Submit — your NTN is generated immediately for salaried individuals. Business registrations may require supporting documents. Your CNIC number itself serves as the NTN for individuals.

The standard deadline for filing annual income tax returns in Pakistan: Individuals and AOPs: September 30 of the assessment year. Companies: December 31 (6 months after the end of the accounting year for companies with a June 30 year-end). FBR frequently extends these deadlines through circulars. Always confirm the current deadline on the FBR website or via AskTax.pk.

For salaried individuals: salary certificate / payslips, bank statements, withholding tax certificate from employer (Form 16 equivalent), CNIC, NTN. For business owners: audited accounts or bookkeeping records, bank statements, asset/liability details, previous tax return. All taxpayers must declare: bank account balances, immovable property, vehicles, investments (shares, mutual funds, prize bonds), and foreign assets.

Under Section 114 of the Income Tax Ordinance, a return must be filed by: every company; every person whose income exceeds the taxable threshold; any person who owns immovable property above 500 sq yards in a rating area or a flat of 2,000 sq ft or more; anyone owning a vehicle of 1,000cc or above; any NTN holder; anyone who paid electricity bills of Rs 500,000 or more in the year; and anyone receiving a fee from a company or AOP. The list was expanded significantly in the 2022 amendments.

Check your ATL (Active Taxpayer List) status via: FBR website → Taxpayer Facilitation → ATL Verification. Or SMS your CNIC to 9966 from any Pakistan mobile number. The ATL is updated weekly (every Monday). You are included in the ATL if you have filed returns for the two most recent tax years. Filing a late return for Rs 1,000 penalty also restores ATL status.

Section 03

Withholding Tax — Key Sections

Section 153 requires every company, AOP, or individual whose accounts are audited to deduct tax before making certain payments. Key rates for filers: Goods: 4.5%, Services: 8%, Contracts: 7%. Non-filers pay double these rates. The withheld amount is treated as advance tax, adjustable against final tax liability. Payments below Rs 75,000 to individuals are generally exempt from withholding.

Section 149 requires every employer to deduct income tax from salary payments on a monthly basis. The employer calculates the estimated annual tax based on the employee's salary, divides it by 12, and deducts that amount each month. Employers must issue an annual withholding certificate to employees and deposit the deducted tax with FBR by the 15th of the following month. Failure to deduct makes the employer liable for the tax.

Section 236C requires advance tax collection on the gross sale consideration of immovable property. The seller pays this tax at the time of property transfer. For filers: 3% of the consideration value. For non-filers: 6%. This is an advance tax, adjustable against final liability. Separate capital gains tax may also apply under Section 37. The registrar/transfer authority is responsible for collecting and depositing this tax.

Under Section 231A, banks deduct advance tax on cash withdrawals exceeding Rs 50,000 per day per account. For filers: 0.6%. For non-filers: 1.2%. This is an advance tax adjustable in the annual return. It is designed to encourage documentation and discourage cash-based transactions. Multiple withdrawals below Rs 50,000 on the same day from the same account are aggregated.

Section 236K requires collection of advance tax on the purchase of immovable property. The buyer pays tax at the time of registering the transfer. For filers: 3%. For non-filers: 10.5%. This high non-filer rate is a key incentive to register and file returns before making property purchases. The tax is adjustable against final income tax liability.

Under Section 156, prize bond winnings and lottery prizes are taxed at 25% for filers and 30% for non-filers, deducted at source. This is a final tax — no further tax is payable on this income and it is not included in the total income for rate purposes. The withholding agent (State Bank, prize bond issuer) deducts before payment.

Section 04

Filer vs Non-Filer — What It Means

Non-filers face higher withholding tax rates on virtually every transaction: double the rate on most withholding sections. Key examples: property purchase (10.5% vs 3%), cash withdrawal (1.2% vs 0.6%), property sale (6% vs 3%), dividend income (20% vs 15%), vehicle registration (higher rates by engine capacity). Non-filers are also barred from purchasing properties above certain values and vehicles above certain CC thresholds.

Steps: (1) Register for NTN via FBR IRIS portal. (2) File your income tax return for the current and past years if not already filed. (3) Pay any late filing penalty (Rs 1,000 per late year). (4) The ATL (Active Taxpayer List) is updated weekly — you will appear as a filer within days of filing. (5) SMS your CNIC to 9966 to verify your status. There is no annual renewal fee — you just need to file each year's return by the deadline.

Section 05

Business & Corporate Income Tax

The standard corporate tax rate is 29% for tax year 2024-25. Small companies (turnover under Rs 250 million) are taxed at 20%. Banking companies pay 39% (including super tax). Companies listed on the Pakistan Stock Exchange that maintain at least 25% free float pay a reduced rate. Special rates apply to manufacturing companies and export-oriented sectors.

Dividend income is taxed as a final tax under Section 150. The withholding rate is 15% for filers and 20% for non-filers. Dividends from money market or income funds are taxed at 25%. Inter-company dividends (between group companies) may qualify for reduced rates. The company paying the dividend deducts the tax at source before distribution.

An Association of Persons (AOP) — including partnerships — is taxed as a separate entity using the individual tax slabs (not the corporate rate). The AOP files its own return. Distributions to individual partners are then exempt from income tax in the partners' hands, provided the AOP has paid tax. AOPs with income from professional services or contracting are common in Pakistan's CA, legal, and construction sectors.

Section 113 imposes a minimum tax of 1.25% on the turnover of companies and registered businesses, regardless of whether they make a profit or loss. This ensures that all active businesses pay some tax. The minimum tax is final if it exceeds the normal tax liability. Certain sectors (importers, traders) have different minimum tax rates. Loss-making companies that pay minimum tax can carry forward the excess as a credit against future tax liability.

Section 06

Sales Tax & GST in Pakistan

The standard federal sales tax rate on goods in Pakistan is 18% (raised from 17% in the Finance Act 2023). Reduced rates apply to certain items: luxury goods may be taxed higher, while basic food items, medicines, and agricultural inputs may be zero-rated or exempt. Retailers pay sales tax at reduced rates under the Tier-1 Retailers scheme.

Businesses with annual taxable supplies or imports exceeding Rs 10 million are required to register for sales tax with FBR. Voluntary registration is allowed below this threshold. Registered persons can claim input tax credits on their purchases, making registration beneficial even for smaller businesses that supply to registered entities. Registration is done through the IRIS portal.

Sales tax on goods is a federal subject, administered by FBR. Sales tax on services was devolved to provinces after the 18th Constitutional Amendment. Each province has its own authority: PRA (Punjab Revenue Authority), SRB (Sindh Revenue Board), KPRA (KP Revenue Authority), and BRA (Balochistan Revenue Authority). Service providers must register with the relevant provincial authority. The standard rate for services is 16% in Punjab and 13% in Sindh, though rates vary by service category.

Section 07

Property & Real Estate Tax

When buying property in Pakistan, the following taxes apply: (1) Advance income tax (Section 236K): 3% for filers, 10.5% for non-filers. (2) Stamp duty: typically 3% of the DC rate value (varies by province). (3) Capital Value Tax (CVT): currently levied in AJK, varies by jurisdiction. (4) Registration fee: 1% of the declared value. These are in addition to any broker fees. Total transaction costs for a filer typically range from 7–10% of the property value.

Pakistan uses two parallel valuation systems for property taxation. The DC Rate (District Collector/Deputy Commissioner rate) is set by provincial governments and used for stamp duty and registration fees — typically well below market value. The FBR Valuation (Section 68) is FBR's own table of property values for major cities, used for income tax purposes (advance tax, capital gains). If the actual transaction price exceeds both, the actual price is used for tax calculations.

Section 08

Capital Gains Tax

Capital gains on immovable property (Section 37) are taxed based on holding period. For open plots: held under 1 year = 15%, 1-2 years = 12.5%, 2-3 years = 10%, 3-4 years = 7.5%, 4-5 years = 5%, 5-6 years = 2.5%, over 6 years = 0%. For constructed property and flats, the holding period for 0% exemption is 4 years. Gain is computed as sale price minus cost of acquisition (and improvement costs).

Capital gains on listed securities (shares, ETFs) are taxed under Section 37A. For gains on shares held less than 1 year: 15%. For shares held 1–2 years: 12.5%. Over 2 years: 0%. Mutual fund gains have separate rates. Losses from securities can be offset against gains in the same year. These rates are designed to discourage short-term speculation while encouraging long-term investment.

Section 09

Overseas Pakistanis & Foreign Income

Overseas Pakistanis who are non-residents (outside Pakistan for more than 183 days in a tax year) are taxed only on their Pakistan-source income — rental income, business income from Pakistan, or property sales. Their foreign salary and foreign-source income is exempt. However, they may still need to file a return in Pakistan if they own property or have other Pakistan-source income. Overseas Pakistanis sending remittances are exempt from tax on those remittances.

As of 2024, Pakistan does not have specific cryptocurrency tax legislation, but the general income tax provisions apply. Gains from cryptocurrency trading are likely taxable as business income or capital gains depending on the nature of the activity. FBR has signaled intent to regulate and tax crypto assets. Taxpayers dealing in crypto should declare such assets and gains in their wealth statements and income tax returns. Undisclosed assets carry significant penalty risk.

Section 10

Penalties, Notices & Appeals

Consequences of not filing: (1) Penalty: Rs 1,000 per day of default or 0.1% of taxable income per week (Section 182). (2) Higher withholding rates on all transactions as a non-filer. (3) Prosecution under Section 192 for willful failure (up to 1 year imprisonment for tax fraud). (4) Exclusion from ATL affecting property, vehicle, and banking transactions. (5) FBR notices under Section 122 for assessment based on third-party data.

FBR sends notices through IRIS portal and registered post. Steps to respond: (1) Log into IRIS and review the notice in "Notices" section. (2) Understand the type: Section 114 (filing notice), Section 122 (amendment), Section 177 (audit), or Section 176 (information request). (3) Respond within the stated timeframe (typically 15–30 days). (4) Submit documents/explanations through IRIS. (5) If you disagree, file a written objection. Ignoring FBR notices leads to ex-parte assessment (they assume worst case).

FBR can select taxpayers for audit under Section 177 (random or risk-based selection) or Section 214C (through the Commissioner). The audit examines whether declared income, expenses, and wealth are accurate. Triggers include: large unexplained wealth, significant lifestyle vs declared income mismatch, industry outliers, prior non-compliance, or random selection. During audit, FBR can request bank statements, invoices, contracts, and asset documentation. Professional representation by a tax lawyer or CA is strongly advised.

Section 11

Provincial Tax Authorities (PRA, SRB, KPRA, BRA)

PRA levies Punjab Sales Tax on Services (PSTS) at a standard rate of 16%. It covers services provided or rendered in Punjab: restaurants, hotels, courier, IT services, consultancy, construction, financial services, and many more. Businesses providing taxable services in Punjab with annual turnover above Rs 3 million must register with PRA. PRA also administers the Punjab Infrastructure Development Cess.

SRB administers Sindh Sales Tax on Services (SSTS) at a standard rate of 13% (reduced from 15% for many categories). Key taxable services: restaurants, hotels, banking, insurance, telecom, advertisements, IT services, freight forwarding, port services, customs agents. The threshold for mandatory registration is lower in Sindh than the federal threshold. SRB has been aggressively expanding the taxable services list and enforcement since 2013.

Section 12

Advanced Topics

Transfer pricing rules under Section 108 of the Income Tax Ordinance require transactions between related parties (associated enterprises) to be conducted at arm's length prices. FBR can re-characterize the price if it deems the declared price is not arm's length. Pakistan follows OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. Multinational companies must maintain transfer pricing documentation. Non-compliance can result in significant tax adjustments and penalties. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) is required for MNCs above a revenue threshold.

Pakistan has signed Double Taxation Avoidance Agreements (DTAAs) with over 65 countries including USA, UK, China, UAE, Saudi Arabia, Germany, France, Canada, Australia, Japan, and most regional neighbors. These treaties prevent the same income from being taxed twice, reduce withholding tax rates on dividends/interest/royalties between treaty countries, and provide dispute resolution mechanisms. Pakistan follows the UN Model Convention in most treaties.

Non-salaried taxpayers whose last year's tax liability exceeded Rs 1 million must pay advance tax in four quarterly installments (September, December, March, June). Each installment is 25% of the previous year's tax. Failure to pay advance tax results in default surcharge (interest) at 12% per annum. Companies also pay advance tax quarterly. Advance tax is credited against the final annual tax liability when the return is filed.

IRIS (Integrated Revenue Information System) is FBR's online taxpayer portal at iris.fbr.gov.pk. Through IRIS you can: register for NTN, file income tax and wealth tax returns, file sales tax returns, respond to FBR notices, view your tax payment history, apply for refunds, check withholding tax statements, and view your tax profile. Most FBR functions that previously required in-person visits are now available on IRIS. Access requires CNIC-based registration.
